US Employment Visas Explained

H-1B Work Visa Pathway

The H-1B is a specialty occupation visa for high-skilled professionals. To hire an H-1B worker, the company must file a Labor Condition Application (LCA) with the Department of Labor stating they will pay the prevailing wage. The H-1B is subject to an annual lottery capping selections at 85,000 cases.

Step 1: Get a Job Offer

Receive an offer from an employer willing to file a petition on your behalf.

Step 2: Enter the H-1B Lottery

Employers register candidates in the online lottery in March.

Step 3: LCA and Form I-129 Filing

Once selected, the employer files a certified LCA and Form I-129 petition with USCIS before the deadline.

OPT & STEM OPT Student Visas

F-1 students graduating from US universities are eligible for 12 months of Optional Practical Training (OPT). Students with qualifying Science, Technology, Engineering, or Math (STEM) degrees can extend their employment authorization for an additional 24 months (STEM OPT).

Critical Prerequisite: E-Verify Registration

To employ a graduate on a STEM OPT extension, the employer MUST be active in the USCIS E-Verify program. Use SponsorTrack's E-Verify badge to filter out companies that are not enrolled.

Reporting Compliance

Students must complete an Form I-983 Training Plan signed by their STEM OPT employer verifying oversight.

Direct Employment (US Citizen / Permanent Resident)

Citizens, Permanent Residents (Green Card holders), and those with unrestricted work authorization (e.g., EAD cards) can work for any employer in the United States. Many major institutions that do not sponsor foreign nationals (e.g. government, defense contractors, small local businesses) are fully open to hiring US Citizens.
